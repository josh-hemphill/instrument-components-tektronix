Numeric frequency/bandwidth messages use ordinary unpadded decimal integers without exponent notation. These software entry limits are conservative: hardware permits 0 Hz center/span and 0.1 Hz frequency resolution, but zero/ fractional requests are deferred so existing neutral positive-number contracts remain unchanged. Do not claim hardware lacks zero span.

For reference level, find integer tenths `n` in -700..400 and require `dbm == n/10.0` using binary64 division. Reject neighboring off-grid values before IO. Format from integer `n` with exactly one fraction digit, e.g. `RL-20.0DM`, `RL0.0DM`, `RL40.0DM`. This stays inside the conservative three-digit remote field despite a four-digit entry statement elsewhere. The allowed -70..40 envelope derives from the commissioned +30 dBm input range: range minus 100 to range plus 10. It is not an RF input-protection bound.

A setter completes its requested write only. RBW/span changes may couple VBW or sweep time; no promise is made that other independent settings stay unchanged. If center/span extends the trace outside 0–40.1 MHz, the instrument may blank those points with SWEEP SPAN LIMITED. No hidden center adjustment is performed to promise an entirely valid trace.

## Marker freshness and side effects

CL clears the display line, making the marker visible without changing its selected point. All other required marker/unit/trace modes are externally commissioned. SP selects the largest point of the **currently stored A trace**, choosing the lower frequency for equal peaks. It does not wait for a new global sweep. MarkerPeak must be documented as stored-trace peak selection. After frequency, reference or scale changes, parts of display storage can still describe an older acquisition until the trace refreshes. Its current displayed coordinates/amplitudes may not represent the original physical points under the previous settings. SP is a display-storage operation, without a fresh/global physical-peak guarantee.

D2 returns frequency plus amplitude. T6 is last and provides delayed valid data at the selected marker without SRQ, rearming/waiting for that marker and settling. MarkerX/Y do not reposition the selected marker and do not promise that every displayed trace point belongs to a newly completed sweep. They parse and validate **both** fields even when returning only one. T6 may hold the bus until the device advances; an independent finite host deadline remains mandatory.

In particular, S2 starts a single sweep and must not be mapped to SweepContinuous(false).

## Exact D2 parser

After stripping only CRLF, the body is exactly 24 ASCII characters: 12-character frequency, comma, 11-character amplitude. Preserve spaces.

- Frequency is a leading `+` or `-`, nine integer positions, point and one fraction digit. The nine integer positions consist of optional leading spaces followed by one or more decimal digits, with no internal/trailing spaces. The parsed value must be 0–40100000 Hz inclusive; negative zero is accepted as zero.
- Amplitude is a leading sign, four integer positions with the same leading-space rule, point, two fraction digits, uppercase `E`, `-`, and exactly `0`. The fixed `E-0` is the documented dB-value exponent. Do not accept another exponent as dBm. Syntax permits the full documented integer field; no unsupported physical amplitude cap is invented.

Reject any wrong width/sign/decimal/comma/exponent, controls, tabs, Unicode, headers, blocks, extra fields or out-of-band frequency as Parse. No overload flag exists in D2, so a numeric response alone cannot prove an unoverloaded/calibrated physical measurement. Return Valid Quantity Hertz/Dbm for X/Y respectively; do not synthesize an overload status.
